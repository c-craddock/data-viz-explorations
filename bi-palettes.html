How popular analytics tools color their charts

The default categorical palettes of seven widely used BI and charting tools, scored with the same checks as our own palettes. Use it to see what each one gets right, and where it breaks down for color-blind readers or on light and dark backgrounds.

Scoring uses each palette's first 8 colors (the full palette is shown), neighbouring pairs, and the same backgrounds as ours: white and grey/1000 #202121. These are our criteria, not the tools' own: several ship separate dark-mode palettes, and Grafana is designed dark-first.

What to take from them

  • Order does as much work as the colors. D3 Category10 and Grafana each put two colors next to each other that look almost the same to red-green color-blind readers (orange/green, purple/violet). The colors are fine; their order is the problem.
  • Soft palettes pay for it in contrast. Tableau 10 and Metabase feel calm and "designed", but 4 and 7 of their first 8 colors sit below 3:1 on white. They depend on labels or tooltips to be readable.
  • Nobody gets light and dark from one set of hex values except Spectrum. IBM Carbon passes every contrast check on white and fails 5 of 8 on dark, which is why Carbon ships a separate dark palette. Power BI goes the other way.
  • Don't put grey in the series order. Superset's slot 5 is #666666, which reads as "Other" or "disabled" and collides with its red for color-blind readers. We keep grey for "Other" only.
  • Leading with blue is the norm. Four of the seven open with blue and a fifth with cyan. Spectrum does too, which keeps our charts familiar.
