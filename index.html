Why the UI steps can't be used as-is

The ramps are luminance-matched: every hue at a given step has the same contrast (all 700s ≈ 5.0:1 on white). That's right for UI, but it means same-step hues differ only in hue. Red, yellow and green collapse for the ~8% of men with red-green color blindness. Chart palettes must mix steps (500, 600, 700) so lightness does part of the work. Turn on a simulation above to see the difference.

There's also a lightness gap between 700 (OKLCH L≈0.55) and 800 (L≈0.38). Nothing in 800–1200 is usable as a series color on light or dark surfaces, so chart colors live in 500–700.

Critical is the system's red/700, which is also Spectrum slot 8. In any chart that shows status, cap Spectrum at seven series (fold the rest into "Other" in grey/600) so a series never impersonates an alert.

Usage rules

  • Assign slots in order, never cycle. Series 1 always gets slot 1. A ninth series becomes "Other" (grey/600, #8b8e89) or a small multiple, never a new hue.
  • Color follows the entity, not its rank. If a filter removes a series, the survivors keep their colors.
  • Scatter, bubble, maps, small multiples: any two marks can touch, so use only the three all-pairs-safe slots.
  • Two or more series always get a legend; four or fewer also get direct labels. Text stays in grey ink, never in the series color.
  • Separate fills with a 2px surface gap (stacked segments, adjacent bars). Lines are 2px; markers ≥ 8px.
  • Use chart chrome from grey: gridlines grey/200 (dark grey/900), baseline grey/300 (dark grey/800), axis labels grey/600.
